Lesson 0b: Subtraction with Blocks

Subtraction is addition backwards. You already know 9 + 4 = 13. So 13 − 9 = 4. We'll use blocks so you can see every fact. We start by taking away 9, then 8, then 7, all the way down to 1.

What taking away looks like 1 · Watch 👀

13 − 9 means: start with 13 blocks. Take 9 away. Count what is left.

The crossed-out blocks are gone. Only 4 solid blocks are left. So 13 − 9 = 4.

Tip: each row holds 10 blocks. The little gap splits each row into 5 and 5, so you can count fast.

Subtraction is addition backwards 1 · Watch 👀

Every addition fact hides two subtraction facts. If 9 + 4 = 13, then 13 − 9 = 4 and 13 − 4 = 9.

9 blue blocks and 4 orange blocks make 13. Take the 9 blue away and the 4 orange are left.

9 + 4 = 13you know this one
4 + 9 = 13same blocks, swapped
13 − 9 = 4take the blue away
13 − 4 = 9take the orange away

Stuck on 13 − 9? Ask yourself: 9 plus what makes 13? The answer is 4.

Count back for small take-aways 1 · Watch 👀

Taking away 1, 2 or 3? Just count back. 12 − 3: start at 12, hop back 3 times: 11, 10, 9.

You land on 9. So 12 − 3 = 9. Say the numbers out loud as you hop.

Jump to 10 for big take-aways 1 · Watch 👀

Taking away a big number like 9? Hop to 10 first. 13 − 9: hop back 3 to land on 10. Then hop back 6 more.

3 + 6 = 9, so you took away 9 in two hops. You land on 4. So 13 − 9 = 4.

10 is a friendly number. Landing on it first makes the rest easy.
