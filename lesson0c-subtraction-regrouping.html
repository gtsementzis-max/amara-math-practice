Lesson 0c: Break a Ten

Big numbers now. 42 − 17 has a problem: you can't take 7 ones from 2 ones. The fix is to break a ten into 10 ones. Teachers call this regrouping (or borrowing). We'll see it with blocks first, then write it in columns.

When there aren't enough ones 1 · Watch 👀

42 − 17. 42 is 4 tens and 2 ones. We need to take away 1 ten and 7 ones. Ones first: 2 − 7? Not enough ones!
1. Start: 42

Only 2 ones. We need 7.

2. Break a ten

One rod becomes 10 cubes. Still 42, just 3 tens and 12 ones.

3. Take away 17

Cross out 7 ones and 1 ten. Left: 2 tens and 5 ones = 25.

So 42 − 17 = 25. Breaking a ten does not change the number. It just makes the ones easy to take away.

Write it in columns 1 · Watch 👀

Same idea, written down. The little purple numbers show the broken ten: 4 tens become 3, and 2 ones become 12.
1
Ones first. 2 − 7? Not enough.
2
Break a ten. Cross out the 4, write 3. The 2 becomes 12.
3
Subtract the ones. 12 − 7 = 5.
4
Subtract the tens. 3 − 1 = 2. Answer: 25.
✓
Check with addition. 25 + 17 = 42. It matches!
⚠️ The trap: don't flip the digits. 7 − 2 = 5 looks easy, but 42 − 17 is not 35. Always break a ten instead.

Three digits: break a hundred too 1 · Watch 👀

325 − 168. Same rule in every column: not enough? Break one from the column to the left.

Ones: 5 − 8? Break a ten: 2 tens become 1, 5 becomes 15. 15 − 8 = 7.

Tens: 1 − 6? Break a hundred: 3 hundreds become 2, 1 becomes 11. 11 − 6 = 5.

Hundreds: 2 − 1 = 1. Answer: 157. Check: 157 + 168 = 325. ✓

When the tens are zero 1 · Watch 👀

405 − 138. Ones: 5 − 8? Not enough. But there are 0 tens to break! So break a hundred first.

Step 1: Break a hundred. 4 hundreds become 3. The 0 tens become 10 tens.

Step 2: Now break a ten. 10 tens become 9. The 5 ones become 15.

Then subtract: 15 − 8 = 7. 9 − 3 = 6. 3 − 1 = 2. Answer: 267.
