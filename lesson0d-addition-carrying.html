Lesson 0d: Make a Ten

Adding big numbers. 47 + 25: the ones make 12. That's too many for one column! So 10 of them bundle into a new ten. Teachers call this carrying (or regrouping). It's the opposite of breaking a ten. Blocks first, then columns.

When the ones make 10 or more 1 · Watch 👀

47 + 25. 47 is 4 tens and 7 ones. 25 is 2 tens and 5 ones. Push them together and count the ones: 7 + 5 = 12.
1. Start: 47 and 25
47
25
2. Push together

6 tens and 12 ones. The dashed cubes are a group of 10.

3. Make a ten

10 cubes became 1 rod (the green one). 7 tens and 2 ones = 72.

So 47 + 25 = 72. Making a ten does not change the number. It just tidies 10 loose ones into one rod.

Write it in columns 1 · Watch 👀

Same idea, written down. The little purple 1 above the tens is the carried ten.
1
Ones first. 7 + 5 = 12.
2
Too big for one column. 12 = 1 ten and 2 ones. Write 2, carry the 1 ten.
3
Tens. Carry first: 1 + 4 + 2 = 7. Answer: 72.
✓
Check by subtracting. 72 − 25 = 47. It matches!
⚠️ Two traps: don't squeeze 12 into the ones column (47 + 25 is not 612), and don't forget the carry (it is not 62 either).

Three digits: carry twice 1 · Watch 👀

358 + 276. Same rule in every column: 10 or more? Write the ones digit, carry the rest to the left.

Ones: 8 + 6 = 14. Write 4, carry 1.

Tens: 1 + 5 + 7 = 13. Write 3, carry 1.

Hundreds: 1 + 3 + 2 = 6. Answer: 634. Check: 634 − 276 = 358. ✓

When the carry needs a new column 1 · Watch 👀

84 + 59. The tens add up to 14. There is no hundreds column yet, so the carried 1 starts one.

Ones: 4 + 9 = 13. Write 3, carry 1.

Tens: 1 + 8 + 5 = 14. Write 4, carry 1.

Hundreds: just the carried 1. Answer: 143. Two-digit numbers can add up to three digits!
