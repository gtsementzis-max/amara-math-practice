Lesson 2 · What is division?

Dragon Egg Division

Mama Dragon needs your help sharing her eggs between the nests. Division is just multiplication asked backwards — let's take it one nest at a time.

🌋

Deep inside Dragon Mountain, Mama Dragon has laid a clutch of eggs. Baby dragons only hatch when the eggs are shared fairly — every nest must hold exactly the same number of eggs. That fair sharing has a math name: division.

🥚 Idea 1: Sharing the eggs equally

Mama Dragon has 12 eggs and 3 nests. If every nest gets the same number, how many eggs go in each nest? You're splitting 12 into 3 equal groups.

3 nests, 4 eggs in each. That's 12 ÷ 3 = 4.

🪺 Idea 2: Making equal nests

Now the same 12 eggs, but this time each nest can only hold 3 eggs. How many nests does Mama Dragon need?

4 nests, 3 eggs in each. That's 12 ÷ 3 = 4 again — same numbers, different question.

Both questions use the same division fact. Sometimes division asks "how many in each nest?" and sometimes it asks "how many nests?" — either way, you divide.

🔥 Now you try: fill the nests

Tap a nest to place one egg in it. Share fairly — every nest must end up with the same number of eggs, or the babies won't hatch!

🐲 The dragon's secret: fact families

You already know your multiplication facts from Lesson 1 — and every multiplication fact secretly hatches two division facts for free. These four facts are called a fact family because they all use the same three numbers.
3 nests × 4 eggs = 12
12 ÷ 3 = 4
4 nests × 3 eggs = 12
12 ÷ 4 = 3
2 nests × 5 eggs = 10
10 ÷ 2 = 5
5 nests × 2 eggs = 10
10 ÷ 5 = 2

So if you ever get stuck on a division problem, ask yourself: "what do I multiply the small number by to get the big number?"

✨ Try the secret (worked examples)

Read each one and see how the multiplication fact hands you the answer.

8 nests × 5 eggs = 40  →  so 40 ÷ 5 = 8  (and 40 ÷ 8 = 5)
