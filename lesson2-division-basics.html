Lesson 2: What Is Division?

Division is just multiplication asked backwards. Let's go through it slowly, one idea at a time.

Idea 1: Sharing equally

Imagine you have 12 cookies and 3 friends. If everyone gets the same number, how many cookies does each friend get? You're splitting 12 into 3 equal groups.

3 friends, 4 cookies each. That's 12 ÷ 3 = 4.

Idea 2: Making equal groups

Now imagine the same 12 cookies, but this time you're packing them into bags of 3. How many bags do you need?

4 bags, 3 cookies in each. That's 12 ÷ 3 = 4 again — same numbers, different question.

Both questions use the same division fact. Sometimes division asks "how many in each group?" and sometimes it asks "how many groups?" — either way, you divide.

The big shortcut: fact families

You already know your multiplication facts from Lesson 1 — and every multiplication fact secretly gives you two division facts for free. These four facts are called a fact family because they all use the same three numbers.
3 × 4 = 12
12 ÷ 3 = 4
4 × 3 = 12
12 ÷ 4 = 3
2 × 5 = 10
10 ÷ 2 = 5
5 × 2 = 10
10 ÷ 5 = 2

So if you ever get stuck on a division problem, ask yourself: "what do I multiply by the small number to get the big number?"
