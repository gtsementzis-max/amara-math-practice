Lesson 5 · Division with remainders

The Leftover Dragon Eggs

Mama Dragon has more eggs than fit evenly in her nests. The eggs that do not fit are the remainder — and today Amara learns to count them.

🥚 What is a remainder?

7 ÷ 3 means share 7 eggs into 3 nests. Each nest gets 2 eggs, and 1 egg is left over. We write 7 ÷ 3 = 2 R1 and say 2 remainder 1.

The remainder is always SMALLER than the number of nests — if it were bigger, you could give every nest one more egg!

⚖️ Two ways to divide: share or group

Sharing

13 eggs shared into 4 nests. How many in each nest?

3 in each nest, 1 left over. 13 ÷ 4 = 3 R1.

Grouping

13 eggs, put 4 in each nest. How many nests can you fill?

3 full nests, 1 left over. 13 ÷ 4 = 3 R1.

Same answer, different question — sharing asks how many in each, grouping asks how many groups.

🐾 Hop back on the number line

Another way: start at the total and hop back by the nest size until you cannot hop any more. Count the hops — that is the answer. Where you land is the remainder.

14 ÷ 4: hop back 4 three times (14 → 10 → 6 → 2). 3 hops, landed on 2. 14 ÷ 4 = 3 R2.
