Lesson 8 · Fractions

The Dragon Feast

At the Dragon Feast every pie is shared FAIRLY — cut into equal slices. A fraction tells you how many of those equal slices you mean. Today: what a fraction is, where it lives on the number line, fractions that look different but are equal, and which of two is bigger.

🥧 What is a fraction? Watch

A dragon pie is cut into 4 equal slices. You eat 3 of them.

You ate 3/4 of the pie.

3

Numerator (top number) = the slices you have. Here: 3.

4

Denominator (bottom number) = the EQUAL slices in the whole pie. Here: 4.

Not a fraction!

These pieces are not equal. So the shaded piece is NOT 1/3. Fractions need EQUAL parts.

One more: 3 equal slices, 2 eaten. That is 2/3. Top = 2 slices you have. Bottom = 3 equal slices in the pie.

📏 A fraction is a number on the line Watch

Cut the space between 0 and 1 into 4 equal jumps. Each jump is 1/4.

Three jumps from 0 lands on 3/4. So 3/4 is a number, and this is where it lives.

1/2 is halfway

2/3 is two jumps of 1/3

4/4 is the whole pie — that is 1. The bigger the bottom number, the SMALLER each jump.

🪞 Same size, different name Watch

The shaded part is the SAME size each time. So 1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8.

Multiply the top and the bottom by the same number. You get an equal fraction: 1/2 × 2/2 = 2/4.

1/3 = 2/6 = 3/9. Same size, different name. These are called equivalent fractions.

⚖️ Which is bigger? Watch

Same bottom number

Same size slices, more of them: 3/5 is bigger.

Same top number

One slice each. But thirds are bigger slices than quarters: 1/3 is bigger.

Different top and bottom

Rename 3/4 as 6/8. Then compare: 6/8 > 5/8, so 3/4 is bigger.

Always look at the picture. The longer shaded bar is the bigger fraction.
